How the triangle and square work

The triangle's top-left corner is white (L=100), bottom-left is black (L=0), and right corner is pure hue (S=100, L=50). Height is lightness. Saturation runs from 0 on the left to 100 at the sloping edge. Horizontal position is chroma: (1 − |2L/100 − 1|) × S/100.

The square uses saturation directly from left (0) to right (100), and lightness from bottom (0) to top (100). It is HSL, like the triangle. Switching shape keeps your colors. An S–L line is straight in the square and can curve in the triangle.

Unlock a surface to arrange individual points, including Base. Uncheck it to lock that arrangement and move the points together: rotation on the hue wheel, translation in the triangle or square. Formula help

Arithmetic: + − * / ^ and parentheses. Functions: min, max, abs, round, sqrt, sin, cos, clamp(value, low, high), wrap(hue). Variables come from the chosen source color.

Channel results round to whole numbers; halves round upward. H wraps; other channels clamp to their valid ranges. CMYK uses an approximate screen conversion, without a print profile.
